## Responses

- **Role, who are you solving for?:** A longtime, highly engaged streaming subscriber and film enthusiast who values quality cinema, variety, and discovering hidden gems.
  
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To quickly discover compelling, thoughtfully selected films that reflect their nuanced tastes rather than simplistic genre preferences.
  
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them:** After spending 20 minutes scrolling through thousands of titles, they encounter repetitive franchise recommendations based on a single viewing choice and close the app without watching anything.
  
- **External tools the user is forced to use:** Returning to physical DVDs, Seeking human-curated recommendations, Using curated competitor services

- **The process, the 3–5 manual steps they take:** 
1. Open StreamLine and browse the catalog: 
The viewer scrolls through thousands of titles, hoping to find a compelling film that matches their interests.

2. Encounter repetitive recommendations: 
The "Because you watched" row presents similar action films or franchise sequels, failing to reflect their broader cinematic tastes.

3. Spend approximately 20 minutes searching without success:
The viewer experiences decision fatigue and loses confidence in the platform's ability to recommend something worthwhile.

4. Abandon StreamLine without starting a film.
Instead of continuing to search, they close the application.

5.Use an alternative discovery or viewing source:
They return to a DVD, or potentially seek curated film selections through a competing service. The DVD behavior is directly observed; competitor switching is documented separately through Tom.
- **Core frustration, where the process feels most "broken":** 
Faced with overwhelming choice and repetitive recommendations the user is forced to abandon the app and seek trusted alternatives for their entertainment
- **The evidence, a quote or behavior from the research:** Tom's interview (UXR-04) provides the strongest direct evidence of this business consequence: he cancelled his subscription and now watches films recommended by a competitor's curated weekly emails.
